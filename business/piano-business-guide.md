# Piano & Music Content Business — Mentor Guide

Budget: max €20/month · Time: 1 hour/day · Start: €0 revenue · Rule: first earnings go back into the business.

Read this whole file once. Then only work on the current phase.

---

## 0. Honest reality check (read first)

- **Most people who try this earn €0–€50/month for the first 3 months, and most quit by month 2.** Your edge is not talent or tools. It is that you keep going while others stop.
- **Views are not money.** The business is: attention → click → trust → purchase. Each step loses most people. Typical rough numbers: out of 1,000 viewers, maybe 10–30 click, and 0–2 buy. These are rules of thumb, not guarantees. Your own numbers matter more.
- **Pure AI slideshows are the weakest version of this idea.** They are cheap, so thousands of people make them, and platforms are tightening rules on low-effort and unlabeled AI content. What wins is a *specific, useful* result for a *specific* kind of learner, ideally with something human in it (you playing, your voice, your real practice).
- **Affiliate income is small and slow.** Your own cheap product earns more per click, but you must make it good.
- **I can't see live platform rules, commissions or algorithm changes.** Verify every platform policy and affiliate term yourself before relying on it. Treat my numbers as starting assumptions, not facts.
- **The real asset you are building is not slideshows.** It is (1) an audience that trusts you, (2) an email list, (3) a product people actually use. Slideshows are only the traffic method for now.

---

## 1. The business in one sentence

> Help **[specific type of learner]** get **[specific result]** in **[short time]**, with free slideshow lessons that lead to a low-priced product.

Example: *"Help adult beginners play 5 recognizable songs in 30 days, with free chord and practice slideshows that lead to a €9–15 practice pack."*

Your first job is to fill in those three brackets yourself (Phase 1). A vague niche ("piano tips for everyone") is the most common reason this fails.

---

## 2. Rules for running it

1. **One niche, one platform first, one product.** Add platforms only after one is working.
2. **Never spend money you can't afford to lose.** Cap: €20/month. No courses, no "guru" programs, no paid growth tricks.
3. **No copyright shortcuts.** Do not sell or post copyrighted sheet music, tabs or full arrangements of songs you don't have rights to. Sell *your own* exercises, public-domain pieces, original arrangements, chord-progression theory, practice plans. Commercial sheet music of pop songs is legally risky.
4. **Disclose affiliate links** (#ad / "affiliate link") as required by the platform and your country's rules.
5. **Label AI-generated content** where the platform asks you to.
6. **Don't buy followers, don't spam comments, don't make fake claims** ("learn piano in 3 days"). It gets accounts banned and costs trust.
7. **Money and tax:** once you earn regularly, you may need to register and report income depending on your country. Check your local tax authority's rules, or ask a gestor/accountant, before your first real payouts. I can't give certain tax advice.
8. **Reinvest rule:** first earnings go into a "business pot" (see section 7). Spend from it only on things in the approved list.

## 4. Phases

### Phase 1: Foundation (Week 1–2) — goal: clarity, not content

Tasks:
1. **Choose your learner.** Pick *one*:
   - adult beginners (no teacher)
   - kids' parents choosing a keyboard
   - returning players who quit as kids
   - pop-song learners who want chords
   - music-theory-curious people
   Choose the one you understand best, because you can speak to them.
2. **Write the one-sentence business** (section 1).
3. **Research 10 accounts** in that niche on one platform. For each, write down: what format, how many followers, which posts got the most engagement, what they sell. Don't copy. Find the gap: what do the top posts *not* cover?
4. **Create accounts** (same handle everywhere if possible). Bio: who it's for + what they get + link in bio.
5. **Set up a free link-in-bio page** (Carrd or Linktree) and a **free email list tool** (e.g. a free tier of MailerLite or similar; check current free limits). The email list is what you own; platforms can disappear.
6. **Start your log** (section 8).

Output of Phase 1: a niche statement, 10 competitor notes, accounts and link page, empty log.

### Phase 2: Learn by publishing (Week 3–6) — goal: 30 posts and data

Tasks:
1. **Make 3 reusable slideshow templates** in Canva (free): *Tip list*, *Step-by-step*, *Song/chord breakdown*.
2. **Post daily on one platform.** 3–4 posts a week should be your best-effort; the rest can be simpler.
3. Mix formats: 40% how-to, 30% mistakes/myths, 20% song/chord, 10% personal (your practice, your story).
4. Each post ends with one call to action: *"Free practice sheet in bio."* Don't pitch a paid product yet.
5. Track every post in the log: hook (slide 1), format, views, likes/saves, clicks, email sign-ups.
6. **Make your free lead magnet** (one-page PDF): e.g. "7 chords every beginner should know" or "15-minute practice routine." This builds your email list.

What matters in this phase: **saves, shares, comments and link clicks**, not just views. A post with 500 views and 20 saves is better than 5,000 views and 0 saves.

Output: 30 posts, a lead magnet, a first set of data about what works.

### Phase 3: Product (Week 7–10) — goal: first product, first sale

1. **Ask your audience what they struggle with.** Use comments and DMs. Their words are your product description.
2. **Build the smallest useful product** (€7–15). Options (use original/public-domain content):
   - "30-day beginner practice planner" (PDF)
   - "Beginner chord progression pack" with original practice patterns
   - "Public-domain easy piano pieces" with fingering and practice notes
3. **Quality check:** test it yourself for a week, and ask 2–3 people to try it and tell you where they got stuck. Fix those spots.
4. **Sell on Gumroad** (or similar). Check fees and payout rules first. Add a clear product page: who it's for, what's inside, what they'll be able to do.
5. **Launch:** one week of posts that show the problem and the product, plus one email to your list.
6. **Add affiliate links** *only* for things you'd honestly recommend and have used or thoroughly researched. Check each program's current terms and approval requirements.

Output: one live product, a product page, first buyers or first learnings about why nobody bought.

### Phase 4: Improve and compound (Month 3–6)

1. Look at the data. Double down on the top 20% of posts: same format, same hook style, new topic.
2. Improve the product based on buyer feedback. Ask buyers one question: *"What almost stopped you from buying?"*
3. Add a **second product only if the first one sells** (e.g. a more advanced pack).
4. Add a second platform by repurposing top posts (Pinterest is good for long-lasting slideshow traffic).
5. Start a simple weekly email to your list: one tip, one link.
6. Consider paying for better tools only from the business pot, and only if it removes a clear bottleneck.

### Decision points (be strict)

- **After Week 6:** if you have 30 posts and <1% of viewers click or save anything, change your **hook and niche angle** before changing platform.
- **After Week 10:** if the product has 0 sales after a real launch, the problem is almost always (a) the audience is too small or (b) the offer doesn't match what they asked for. Fix those, not the price.
- **After Month 6:** if there's no growth in email sign-ups, clicks or sales, either narrow the niche further or pivot to the "done-for-you content for local music teachers/schools" option from earlier.

## 7. Money tracker (keep it simple)

Keep one spreadsheet with three tabs:

1. **Income:** date, source (product/affiliate), amount.
2. **Costs:** date, what, amount.
3. **Business pot:** running total = income − costs not yet reinvested. First earnings stay here.

Targets (not promises): first €1 · first €20 · first month with income > costs. Celebrate those, but judge the business by trends over months.

## 8. Weekly review (every Sunday, 45 min instead of the daily block)

Answer in writing:

1. Which 3 posts did best? What do they have in common (hook, topic, format)?
2. Which 3 did worst? Why do you think so?
3. Numbers: posts made, total views, saves, link clicks, email sign-ups, sales, money in/out.
4. What did I learn this week?
5. What will I do differently next week? (**Max 2 changes**, otherwise you can't tell what worked.)
6. Did I actually do the hour each day? If not, what blocked me?

Send it to me and I'll respond with feedback.

---

## 9. Common mistakes to avoid

- Changing niche every two weeks.
- Posting only AI-generated stuff with no personality or specificity.
- Making a product before you know what your audience wants.
- Copying commercial sheet music or song arrangements.
- Judging by views only.
- Buying tools to feel productive.
- Waiting to be "ready." The first 30 posts will be mediocre; that's normal.
